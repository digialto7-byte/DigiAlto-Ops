### 1.2 Research: current online presence (R01; research, not client facts)

- **The name differs across listings.** The owner's own menu says "Osteria dei Rossi". Google-data mirrors, TripAdvisor, Quandoo, PagineGialle and the Instagram display name say "L'Osteria dei Rossi". Gambero Rosso, Frommer's, Falstaff and the indexed Facebook page say "L'Osteria". Yelp and Foursquare say "Osteria L'Osteria". That makes at least six spellings, and the street number is often cut to "79". Source: R01 §8 NAP table, e.g. [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g187902-d716270-Reviews-L_Osteria_dei_Rossi-Siena_Tuscany.html), [PagineGialle](https://www.paginegialle.it/l-osteria-dei-rossi-siena), [Yelp](https://www.yelp.com/biz/osteria-l-osteria-siena).
- **No own site ranks.** A brand search returns only third-party pages. `menu.osteriadeirossi.it` is not indexed, is built with JavaScript, and has no structured data, robots.txt or sitemap. No listing we could read links to an own website. R01 §5, [menu site](https://menu.osteriadeirossi.it/).
- **Listings disagree with each other and with the approved hours.** Sunday shows as closed on TripAdvisor, Quandoo, PagineGialle and others, but open at lunch on the Google mirrors; most listings show Monday open. Two emails and a second phone number (+39 0577 1460316, on Wheree) circulate. There are three Facebook URLs (`/osteriadeirossi`, `/OsteriaSiena`, `/losteria.siena/`). OpenTable shows province "TO" and Yelp shows "Deux-Sèvres". R01 §8. Against P-L41, the Monday and Sunday entries on most listings are now plainly wrong.
- **Review themes** (internal only: no rating, count, ranking or quote goes into copy). Guests praise the pici (cacio e pepe, ragù bianco di Cinta), the wild boar, the bis di crostoni, the friendly young staff, the rustic vaulted room and the tables on the street. Complaints are about price against portion size (2025 to 2026), how the steak is priced, a bottle of red served too cold, a perceived decline after the management change, and slow service at peak. One management reply was seen (13 January 2026). R01 §2.
- **Booking:** no online booking platform was found; Quandoo says the osteria is not bookable there. R01 §3.

## 4. Plan

### 4a. Positioning (SVC-MKT-01)

**Positioning sentence**
- IT: *Un'osteria nella Contrada del Bruco, in Via dei Rossi, dentro le mura di Siena: cucina senese con radici nel Medioevo, dal 1992.*
- EN: *An osteria in the Bruco contrada, on Via dei Rossi inside Siena's walls: Sienese cooking with medieval roots, since 1992.*

**Three proof points**

| Proof | Facts it rests on |
|---|---|
| 1. The place: a street with a name and a contrada | Via dei Rossi runs from Banchi di Sopra to Piazza San Francesco and is named after the Rossi family's 13th-century castellare; the osteria is on the Bruco's side; Porta Ovile is the Bruco's gate; the Basilica di San Francesco is at the end of the street; Piazza del Campo is about 5 minutes on foot; the centre is UNESCO World Heritage since 1995 (P-L89 to L91, L70, L94) |
| 2. Continuity, with people's names | Opened in 1992 by Giuseppe "Beppe" Carrieri, brucaiolo; Luca and Max since early 2024, Luca in the kitchen; Spaghetti alla Chitarra con Sugo dell'Osteria "in menù dal 1992", as printed on the approved menu (P-L84, L38) |
| 3. The table and the cellar | Cinghiale in dolceforte, a sweet-and-sour sauce that goes back to medieval cooking (the chocolate came later); cinghiale in umido in terracotta; pici; fresh truffle all year; taglieri; fiorentina in different cuts and breeds, chosen weekly by Alessio (approved menu text); Cinta Senese from the Crete Senesi (approved menu text); listed by Gambero Rosso among its chosen addresses for meat in Siena; a brick-vaulted grotto that leads to the cellar; Chianti, Rosso di Montepulciano, Montalcino; amari (P-L65, L66, L72, L75, L88, L93, L38) |

**What we deliberately do not claim**
- No ratings, review counts or rankings. No "best", "oldest", "the only" or "number one". **Gambero Rosso** appears **once** on the site, as a sober statement of fact: the osteria is among the addresses for meat chosen in the article "Dove mangiare un'ottima carne a Siena". It is never written as "best", never in a title, meta description or H1, and never as a badge. No other guide or award is mentioned.
- No claim that today's dolceforte is a medieval recipe unchanged. The approved line (P-L93) is: the sweet-and-sour principle goes back to medieval cooking, and chocolate was added in later centuries. "Dishes from the Middle Ages" (P-L64) is not stretched to every dish: research shows "ribollita" as a name is first printed in 1910 and "bistecca" is a 19th-century loanword (R05 §4).
- No Chianina claim for the fiorentina: the approved menu speaks of breeds and farms "from around the world".
- No play on "Rossi" as the colour red, and no "famiglia Rossi" running the osteria. Rossi is the medieval family the street is named after (R05 §1). **Challenge to the handover:** 01_STRUCTURE §4b suggests "the Rossi family" and the headline "Prenota il tuo tavolo / *dai Rossi*". Both imply owners called Rossi, so both are dropped.
- No official tie to the Contrada del Bruco (sponsorship, "the contrada's osteria"). We say the osteria is *in* the Bruco, and the contrada's emblem and colours are not used as brand elements.
- No Etruscan origin for pici, no dates for the building or its vaults, no "1975", no email, no second phone number, no parking tariff, no walking time from the car park (none of these is approved).
- No promise about opening on Palio days or race times. P-L94 gives the dates only; research shows races can slip a day or two for rain (R05 §6).
- No claim that the osteria stands at or near Porta Ovile. The gate is the contrada's gate and the site's opening image, not the osteria's door.

### 4c. Voice (SVC-MKT-01)

**Three adjectives:** schietta (plain-spoken), ospitale (hospitable), precisa (exact about facts and origins).

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Name things: brick, terracotta, lantern, coccio, Cinta Senese, Crete Senesi, Via dei Rossi | Superlatives, or "autentico", "tipico", "unico" unless the noun after them is a fact |
| Two-line headlines: a plain first line, then an italic completion starting with a preposition; 2 to 4 words per line; sentence case; no full stops in titles | Exclamation marks, rhetorical questions, emojis |
| 1 to 2 sentences per block, 25 to 40 words | Repeating a paragraph or a fact across sections |
| Describe the room by materials and light, the experience by time, quality by origin | Ratings or awards; Gambero Rosso more than once or as "best" |
| Use the menu's own dish names exactly | Inventing dish history or dating the building |
| Keep the button and link labels identical across pages: **Prenota / Book a table**, **Chiama / Call**, **WhatsApp**, **Guarda il menù / See the menu** | Mixing "Menu" and "Menù" in Italian (Italian text: "menù"; URL: `/menu/`) |

**Italian address: "voi".** The osteria's own menu already speaks in "voi" ("Mettetevi comodi", "lasciateci due righe", R01 §5). It suits a table of several people, and it reads as Tuscan hospitality rather than marketing familiarity. Use "voi" everywhere and never mix it with "tu".

**English:** British spelling and usage (flavour, centre, "book a table", "starters", "mains"). Italian dish names stay in Italian, followed by an English description.

**Headline patterns** (patterns only; the copywriter writes the actual lines):
1. `[Luogo] / *dentro le mura*` (a place noun + the governing idea)
2. `[Nome della via] / *da [punto] a [punto]*` (a street as a route)
3. `[Materiale] e [luce] / *sotto le volte*` (the room through its materials)
4. `[Piatto] / *dal Medioevo*` or `/ *nel coccio*` (a dish + its origin or its vessel)
5. `[Anno] / *in [luogo]*` (a dated fact anchored to a place)
6. `Prenota / *per telefono o WhatsApp*` (an action + the channel)

### 4d. Identity direction (SVC-MKT-01, SVC-WEB-02)
