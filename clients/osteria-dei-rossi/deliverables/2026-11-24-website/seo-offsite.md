## 1. What the website can and cannot do for the Google Map Pack

- The Map Pack (the three local results with a map) is decided mainly by the Google Business Profile, by how far the searcher is from the place, and by how well known the place is, which includes the number and quality of real reviews and mentions elsewhere. Google describes these as relevance, distance and prominence.
- The website **can**: make the profile's data consistent (same name, address, phone and hours everywhere), give Google one clear page to read about the place (home, menu, FAQ, structured data), receive the profile's website, menu and booking links, and earn a place in ordinary results and in answers from AI assistants for narrow queries (near the Basilica di San Francesco, the Contrada del Bruco, dish names).
- The website **cannot**: change the distance between the guest and the door, create reviews, replace the profile, or lift a place into the Map Pack by itself.
- Third-party mirrors suggest the osteria already has a Google profile with a large review base (about 1,000 to 1,140 reviews at about 4.5 in snapshots of different ages; to be checked on the live profile). These figures are for internal analysis only and must never be copied into anything public. The existing profile and its history are the most valuable local asset. **Never create a second profile.** Claim or request access to the existing one (the Google Maps link in the profile is `https://maps.google.com/?cid=5066073290586870112`) and edit it.
- The off-site work below is therefore the larger lever in the first months, and the website is the base it points to. Results take weeks to months, vary by searcher and place, and are measured in sections 6 and 7, not promised.

### 2.2 Business name

Set the name to exactly: **Osteria dei Rossi**
Reasons to change it from "L'Osteria dei Rossi" (the form that mirrors, TripAdvisor, Quandoo, PagineGialle and Instagram show; to be checked on the live profile):
1. The founder's decision (profile, Identity): the public name for the website and every listing is "Osteria dei Rossi", lower-case "dei", no "L'".
2. One name string everywhere lets search engines and AI assistants join the profile, the website and the listings into one entity. The research found at least seven spellings.
3. "L'Osteria" collides with a national restaurant chain of the same name in search results.
4. Google expects the profile name to match the name the business uses in the real world. The website, the menu and the Instagram handle say "Osteria dei Rossi"; the street sign says "Osteria".

How: edit the name in the profile (owner or manager only). Do not add keywords, the city or a dish to the name; that is a policy violation and can get the profile suspended. A name change on a profile with many reviews can occasionally trigger a re-check by Google; do the edit once, together with the hours and categories, and keep the evidence ready (photo of the sign, the website).

## 4. Review routine

**Principles.** Ask every guest the same way; never choose who to ask by how the meal went; never reward, discount or thank with something in return for a review; never write or script a review; never post a review for the osteria from staff, relatives or the premises' own devices. Google and TripAdvisor both prohibit these, and fake or incentivised reviews are never part of this work. No follow-up messages: a WhatsApp booking number is not an invitation to send marketing afterwards.

**QR card.**
- Put the QR code (the profile's "Ask for reviews" link) on a small card in the bill folder and, discreetly, on the table. Test it on an iPhone and an Android phone before printing.
- Timing: at the bill, after the guest has paid; never mid-meal.
- Staff may point to the card if a guest asks how to leave an opinion. They do not ask only the table that looked happy.
- Card wording, the same for everyone. Italian: `Com'è andata? Se volete, potete lasciare il vostro parere su Google, nella lingua che preferite. Basta inquadrare il codice.` English: `How was your visit? If you like, you can leave your own review on Google, in the language you prefer. Just scan the code.` German (when German pages exist): `Wie war Ihr Besuch? Wenn Sie möchten, können Sie auf Google Ihre eigene Bewertung abgeben, in der Sprache Ihrer Wahl. Einfach den Code scannen.`
- The live menu page currently asks "Se vi siete trovati come a casa, lasciateci due righe. Per noi una recensione vale quanto un bis." That wording invites only satisfied guests, which counts as selective asking. Replace it with the neutral wording above when the page is next edited.
- No tablet or review station in the room (many reviews from one network are filtered).

**Replying.** Reply to every review, positive or negative, in the language the guest wrote in, within a few days. Be brief and specific. Never argue, never blame the guest, never mention bills, discounts or compensation in public (a January 2026 TripAdvisor reply found by the research discussed a bill reduction in public; avoid that), never offer anything in exchange for changing or removing a review, never use the same text twice. Invite the guest to call or write on WhatsApp for anything personal. Sign as the team. Only write what is true. If a review looks fake, report it through the profile instead of answering it.

Pattern 1, praise:

## 7. Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools and monthly report

### 7.1 Google Search Console

1. Add a **Domain** property for `osteriadeirossi.it` (DNS TXT record at the registrar). A domain property covers `www` and the `menu` subdomain. Add the owner and one DigiAlto user.
2. After launch, submit `https://www.osteriadeirossi.it/sitemap.xml`.
3. Use URL Inspection on the six URLs (live test first), then request indexing for Home IT, Home EN, Menu IT and Menu EN.
4. Check Pages (indexing): all six indexed; the 404 not indexed; the old preview host not indexed.
5. Check Enhancements: Restaurant, Menu, FAQ and breadcrumb items parse without errors (rich results are not promised).
6. Check Core Web Vitals after enough real visits (a small site may show no data for weeks).
7. Weekly in the first month, then monthly: Performance by query, page, country and device.

### 7.2 Bing Webmaster Tools

1. Import the site from Search Console or verify by DNS.
2. Submit the same sitemap; check indexing and the SEO reports.
3. Connect Bing Places (section 3) so the place data matches.

### 7.3 Measurement without tracking scripts

The site has no analytics and sets no cookie, by decision. Use: Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, the Google profile's Performance data (searches, calls, direction requests, website clicks), TripAdvisor's management data, and a simple tally by staff of "how did you find us?" when a guest books. Server logs from the host can show page requests but not people. If the owner later approves cookie-free analytics, add it then and record the decision in the profile first.

### 7.4 Monthly report outline (one page)

1. Summary in five lines: what changed, what moved, what to do next.
2. Search Console: clicks, impressions, average position for brand and non-brand queries; top ten queries and pages; Italian against English; countries.
3. Rank sheet: the 30 queries, movement since last month, map pack yes or no, AI answers checked.
4. Google profile: searches, calls, direction requests, website clicks; posts published; hours checked.
5. Reviews: new reviews this month, replies sent and pending, repeated themes (internal only; no rating markup, no quotes on the site).
6. Listings and citations: corrected this month, still wrong, links gained.
7. Technical: indexing status, Core Web Vitals, structured-data errors, Lighthouse spot check.
8. Content published and planned (section 8).
9. Open questions for the owner.
No figure is a promise; the report states what was measured and where.
