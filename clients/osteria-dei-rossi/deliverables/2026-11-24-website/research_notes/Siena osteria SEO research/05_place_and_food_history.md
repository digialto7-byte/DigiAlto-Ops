## 1. Via dei Rossi: route, name, neighbours, terzo, walking distances, parking, ZTL

### Takeaway
Via dei Rossi runs from Banchi di Sopra (under the Arco dei Rossi) downhill to Piazza San Francesco, and Italian state heritage records derive its name from the 13th-century castellare of the Rossi family at its mouth (today Palazzo Bichi Ruspoli). Booking-site estimates for the house next door (no. 77) put Piazza del Campo at about 300 m / 3-5 minutes and the Duomo at about 600 m / 7-8 minutes; the nearest car park is San Francesco in Via Baldassarre Peruzzi, linked to the centre by escalators, and the whole walled centre is a camera-controlled ZTL.

### Cited Findings

**Route and character**
- The contrada's own street list defines the street's extent as "via dei Rossi lato sinistro, da Banchi di Sopra a Piazza San Francesco", i.e. the street runs from Banchi di Sopra to Piazza San Francesco. Confidence: well documented. — [Nobil Contrada del Bruco, La Contrada](https://www.nobilcontradadelbruco.it/la-contrada/); same wording on [ilpalio.siena.it, Bruco - Territorio](https://www.ilpalio.siena.it/5/Contrade/Bruco?territorio=&lang=en)
- A walking guide to the Bruco describes the street as descending: "Follow Via dei Rossi downhill until the road comes to a Y, and stay right onto Piazza di San Francesco." Confidence: single source (attribution between the two walking guides to confirm). — [Siena Walks 08: Nobile Contrada del Bruco](https://stqry.app/projects/808/tour/2221/preview)
- A second guide gives the approach from Banchi di Sopra: "Turn left onto Salvani Provenzano and then right onto Via dei Rossi, which will take you to the church of San Francesco." Confidence: single source. — [Discover Tuscany, Walks in Siena](https://discovertuscany.com/siena/walks-in-siena.html)
- Via del Refe Nero opens off Via dei Rossi (the Giraffa's share of the street is "lato destro da via del Refe Nero a piazza San Francesco"), and Via dell'Abbadia runs "da via dei Rossi a piazza dell'Abbadia". Confidence: well documented. — [ilpalio.siena.it, Giraffa - Territorio](https://www.ilpalio.siena.it/5/Contrade/Giraffa?territorio=&lang=en); [Nobil Contrada del Bruco, La Contrada](https://www.nobilcontradadelbruco.it/la-contrada/)
- A local-history series records a lane (vicolo degli Orbachi) that ran "dietro alle case di via dei Rossi, sbucava in piazza dell'Abbadia". Confidence: single source. — [ilpalio.org, Osterie e locande dei secoli passati](https://www.ilpalio.org/pagina2.htm)

**Origin of the name (documented, not legend)**
- Italian state heritage catalogue (ICCD) record for the palace at "via dei Rossi 1/3/5": Palazzo Bichi Ruspoli is also called the castellare dei Rossi, "da cui deriva il nome l'omonima via laterale"; the building stands beside the Arco dei Rossi and is joined by a passage over the street to the buildings that formed "il castellare dei Rossi, edificato nella prima metà del secolo XIII" along the Via Francigena, which crossed the city here. Confidence: well documented (state catalogue; which of the two record numbers carries each sentence to confirm). — [ICCD record 10052028](https://sigecweb.beniculturali.it/sigec/item/print/ICCD10052028); [ICCD record 9000621](https://sigecweb.beniculturali.it/sigec/item/print/ICCD9000621)
- The same records say the Bichi Ruspoli family remodelled the palace in the 1700s, that the Arco dei Rossi "presenta stucchi in stile tardo barocco", and that the palace was reworked again between the 18th and 19th centuries. Confidence: well documented. — [ICCD record 10052028](https://sigecweb.beniculturali.it/sigec/item/print/ICCD10052028)
- English Wikipedia (secondary): "Palazzo Bichi Ruspoli, or previously Palazzo or Castellare dei Rossi, is an urban palace, located on Via Banchi di Sopra"; it originally belonged to the Rossi family, housed Charles of Anjou during a stay in Siena, and stands between Palazzo Salimbeni to the north and Palazzo Tolomei to the south. Confidence: single source for the Charles of Anjou detail. — [Wikipedia, Palazzo Bichi Ruspoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Bichi_Ruspoli)

**Terzo**
- Wikipedia places Palazzo Bichi Ruspoli (the building at the mouth of Via dei Rossi) in the Terzo di Camollia. Confidence: single source; the same sentence assigns the palace to the Civetta contrada, which conflicts with the contrada street lists below (the Bruco lists the right side of Banchi di Sopra from Via dei Rossi to Piazza Salimbeni). — [Wikipedia, Palazzo Bichi Ruspoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Bichi_Ruspoli); contradicted on the contrada by [Nobil Contrada del Bruco, La Contrada](https://www.nobilcontradadelbruco.it/la-contrada/)

Confidence: well documented (dates of the facade works vary slightly between sources). — [Wikipedia, Basilica of San Francesco, Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Francesco,_Siena); [Frommer's, San Francesco](https://www.frommers.com/destinations/siena/attractions/san-francesco)

Confidence: well documented. — [Wikipedia, Basilica of San Francesco, Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Francesco,_Siena)

### Inferences
- Via dei Rossi is in practice the short link between the Banchi di Sopra spine (the urban Via Francigena) and the Franciscan complex: a guest walking from Piazza del Campo reaches it in a few minutes, and a guest arriving by car reaches it from the opposite end, via the San Francesco escalators.
- Which side of the street is no. 79/81? The catalogue places nos. 1/3/5 at the Banchi di Sopra end, so numbering starts there. Under the usual Italian convention (odd numbers on the left from the start of the street; convention not sourced in this session) 79/81 would be on the left-hand side, which is the side the Bruco lists as its own. This supports the owner's statement but is an inference; confirm on site (contrada wall tiles) or with the contrada.
- The "Banca Monte dei Paschi di Siena" point in the hotel listings is almost certainly the bank's seat on Piazza Salimbeni, which would put Piazza Salimbeni about 2-3 minutes from no. 77; the listing does not name the square, so treat as to confirm.
- "Rossi" in the street name is a family name, not the colour red; any play on "red" in copy is wordplay, not etymology.

## 2. Nobile Contrada del Bruco

### Takeaway
The contrada's official street list includes the left side of Via dei Rossi from Banchi di Sopra to Piazza San Francesco (the right side from Via del Refe Nero onward belongs to the Giraffa), its seat, museum and oratory are in Via del Comune (no. 44), and its gate is Porta Ovile. Its "Nobile" title is tied by official sources to the fight at the Croce del Travaglio in January 1369 and to the July 1371 rising of the Compagnia del Bruco led by Barbicone; its last Palio win is 16 August 2008 (37 official wins), still the case after both 2026 races.

### Cited Findings

**Name, emblem, colours, motto**
- Official name used by the Comune and the Palio consortium site: "Nobile Contrada del Bruco"; the contrada's own website uses "Nobil Contrada del Bruco". Confidence: well documented. — [Comune di Siena, Bruco](https://palio.comune.siena.it/node/5800); [nobilcontradadelbruco.it](https://www.nobilcontradadelbruco.it/la-contrada/)
- Emblem (official blazon): "Un Bruco incoronato alla Granducale, striscia su di un ramoscello al naturale, fogliato di verde e illeggiadrito da un bocciolo di rosa." Confidence: well documented. — [nobilcontradadelbruco.it, La Contrada](https://www.nobilcontradadelbruco.it/la-contrada/); [Comune di Siena, Bruco](https://palio.comune.siena.it/node/5800)
- Colours: "giallo e verde listato di azzurro" (official); a cultural magazine words it "giallo e verde con liste turchine". Confidence: well documented. — [nobilcontradadelbruco.it, La Contrada](https://www.nobilcontradadelbruco.it/la-contrada/); [Finestre sull'Arte](https://www.finestresullarte.info/opere-e-artisti/gli-animali-simbolo-delle-contrade-di-siena-dove-si-trovano)

Confidence: well documented that both circulate; use the official form unless the contrada says otherwise. — [nobilcontradadelbruco.it, La Contrada](https://www.nobilcontradadelbruco.it/la-contrada/); [Comune di Siena, Bruco](https://palio.comune.siena.it/node/5800); alternative in [thepalio.eu, Bruco](https://www.thepalio.eu/contrade/bruco/) and [ilpalio.siena.it](https://www.ilpalio.siena.it/amp/Contrade/Bruco.amp.html?lang=en) (attribution to confirm)

**1369, 1371 and the title "Nobile"**
- Official account: the title derives from the part its people took in the clash of January 1369 at the Croce del Travaglio and from the 1371 rising. Francesco d'Agnolo, called Barbicone, led the Compagnia del Bruco with Giovanni di Monna Tessa and Domenico di Lano; the revolt proper began on 14 July 1371 when the Palazzo del Senatore was stormed. Confidence: well documented as the official account. — [nobilcontradadelbruco.it, La Storia](https://www.nobilcontradadelbruco.it/la-contrada/la-storia/); [Comune di Siena, Bruco](https://palio.comune.siena.it/node/5800)
- Secondary wording: the title "was earned in 1369 by its people's bravery in helping to defeat Charles IV, and consolidated in 1371 when they led the revolt to replace the Sienese council with a people's government"; the rebels "wanted peace and bread". Confidence: single source each. — [Wikipedia, Contrade of Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrade_of_Siena); [thepalio.eu](https://www.thepalio.eu/?p=237)
- Scholarly anchor: Franco Franceschi wrote "La rivolta di Barbicone" (in Storia di Siena, 1995) and notes that the Sienese revolt of summer 1371, seven years before the Florentine Ciompi, is documented by a single contemporary narrative, a Sienese chronicle (author's name truncated in the extract, to confirm). A French research database dates the Siena event to July 1371. Confidence: well documented that the event happened; details rest on one chronicle. — [Franceschi, storiadifirenze.org](https://www.storiadifirenze.org/pdf_ex_eprints/157-Franceschi.pdf); [HiSCoD, Université de Caen](https://www.unicaen.fr/hiscod/fr/italie/conflits_sociaux96.xml/ead/HISCOD_16002fr.pdf)
- Context: in the 1370s and 1380s political movements in Florence, Siena, Lucca and Perugia drew in wage labourers, mostly wool workers, and small artisans. Confidence: single source (abstract). — [Poloni, Università di Pisa](https://arpi.unipi.it/handle/11568/809989)

**Allies, rivals**
- Allies listed: Istrice (since 1665), Nicchio and Torre (since 1912). Former alliances: Chiocciola (1905-2002), Leocorno (1937-1995), Lupa (until 1963). No current rival; the traditional enmity with the neighbouring Giraffa ended formally in 1996. Confidence: single source for the dates. — [thepalio.eu, Bruco](https://www.thepalio.eu/contrade/bruco/); [Wikipedia, Contrade of Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrade_of_Siena)

**Palio record**

Confidence: well documented. — [Comune di Siena, Bruco](https://palio.comune.siena.it/node/5800); [nobilcontradadelbruco.it, Le Vittorie](https://www.nobilcontradadelbruco.it/la-contrada/le-vittorie/)

Confidence: well documented. — [AGI, 3 July 2026](https://www.agi.it/cronaca/news/2026-07-03/palio-siena-vince-aquila-37858257/); [Sky TG24, 18 August 2026](https://tg24.sky.it/cronaca/2026/08/18/palio-siena-2026-vincitore); [La Nazione, draw](https://www.lanazione.it/siena/cronaca/palio-luglio-contrade-estratte-vhcwv5xo)

**Gate**
- Porta Ovile lies in the Bruco's territory: the Bando clause begins "Dalla Porta Ovile" and the street list runs "via di Vallerozzi lato destro (da piazza d'Ovile a Porta Ovile)"; the contrada describes its historic area as the district around Porta d'Ovile, a gate that "nel 1555 fu murata e in parte interrata" to withstand bombardment. Confidence: well documented. — [nobilcontradadelbruco.it, Il Territorio](https://www.nobilcontradadelbruco.it/il-territorio/); [Fonti del Palio, Bruco](https://www.fontidelpalio.org/documenti-fondamentali/bando-di-violante/bruco/)

### Inferences
- Read together, the two Bando clauses and the modern street lists say the same thing: walking from Banchi di Sopra toward San Francesco, the left (north) side of Via dei Rossi is Bruco and the right side, from Via del Refe Nero on, is Giraffa. A business on Via dei Rossi can say it is "in the Bruco" only if it stands on the Bruco side; see the numbering inference in part 1.
- The 1729/1730 discrepancy may simply reflect decree versus promulgation dates (or old-style year reckoning); the sources found do not explain it, so copy should say "the Bando of 1729-1730" or cite the Comune's "1729".
- The 1371 rising is documented history, but narrative colour (speeches, motives, the wool-workers label) rests on one chronicle and later contrada tradition; writers should present the event as fact and the detail as "according to the contrada's tradition".

- No source found in this session states the Bruco's link to wool workers or silk workers in so many words (one unreliable page says silk); the contrada's "La Storia" page should settle it.
- Year the "Nobile" title was formally conferred and by whom: not found.
- Exact start dates of the Nicchio alliance, and confirmation of all alliance dates on an official page: not found.

## 3. The city gates (Porta Ovile in detail; Camollia, Romana, Pispini for comparison)

### Takeaway
Porta Ovile is a 13th-century brick gate (completed 1246 per Italian Wikipedia; other pages say c. 1230 or "documented from 1220"), reworked in the 14th century, low in proportion, with a pointed arch and a crenellated forework (antemurale) whose inner wall carries a Madonna and Child attributed to Sano di Pietro; the medieval Fonte d'Ovile stands just outside. It is the only gate inside the Bruco's territory, which makes it the natural gate for a Via dei Rossi address; Porta Camollia (1604 rebuild, Medici arms, "Cor magis tibi Sena pandit") is the famous welcome gate but belongs to the Istrice.

### Cited Findings

**Porta Ovile**
- "Completata nel 1246 e rimaneggiata nel Trecento, è tra le meglio conservate della città. Ha un antemurale merlato, nella cui parete interna si trova un affresco con la Madonna col Bambino di Sano di Pietro." It stands in the Borgo d'Ovile between Via di Vallerozzi and Via Simone Martini. Confidence: well documented for the description; date see next line. — [Wikipedia IT, Porta a Ovile](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_a_Ovile); [Wikipedia IT, Borgo d'Ovile](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borgo_d'Ovile)
- Date variants: "La costruzione risale al 1230, e successivamente ebbe diversi ristrutturazioni"; "Documented from 1220"; Google Arts & Culture labels its image "1230/1471"; Frommer's calls it a "14th-century double city gate". Give "13th century, reworked in the 14th". — [Niceplaces](https://www.niceplaces.it/siena/porta-ovile); [Hotel Minerva, Porta Ovile](https://www.albergominerva.it/en/porta-ovile-a-siena/); [Google Arts & Culture, Porta Ovile](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/porta-ovile/WQG-M6c378m5iQ); Frommer's (one of two pages, attribution to confirm): [San Francesco](https://www.frommers.com/destinations/siena/attractions/san-francesco) or [Oratorio di San Bernardino](https://www.frommers.com/destinations/siena/attractions/oratorio-di-san-bernardino-e-museo-diocesano)
- Materials and shape: brick with battlements; "a large pointed arch flanked by a crenellated antemurale (small bastion)"; it retains the crenellated antemural forming a small defensive outwork. Confidence: well documented (several guides agree). — [Hotel Minerva, historic gates](https://www.albergominerva.it/en/porta-camollia-and-the-other-historic-gates-of-siena/); [Hotel Minerva, Porta Ovile](https://www.albergominerva.it/en/porta-ovile-a-siena/)
- Proportions: its structure is "much lower" than the other gates; one guide says the street level was raised in the 1600s because of flooding, another attributes the lowering to later road and bridge works. Confidence: single source each, explanations differ. — [Hotel Minerva, Porta Ovile](https://www.albergominerva.it/en/porta-ovile-a-siena/); [Visit Italy, Porta Ovile](https://www.visititaly.it/info/961358-porta-ovile-siena.aspx)
- Tabernacle: "All'interno dell'anticamera difensiva si trova un tabernacolo con un affresco gotico della Madonna col Bambino attribuito a Sano di Pietro, ancora visibile sul muro interno." One guide gives the subject as "Madonna col Bambino, fra i SS. Ansano e Bernardino e gli Angeli". Confidence: well documented for the attribution; exact subject single source. — [Hotel Minerva, historic gates (IT)](https://www.albergominerva.it/quali-sono-le-porte-storiche-di-siena-camollia-romana-e-altre/); [Visit Italy](https://www.visititaly.it/info/961358-porta-ovile-siena.aspx)
- Further images reported on the inner arch: two niches, one with a 16th-century tabernacle painted by Bartolomeo di David (Madonna and Child with the young St John and St Catherine of Siena), the other with a Madonna and Child of the early 14th century attributed to Memmo di Filippuccio. Confidence: single source. — [Niceplaces](https://www.niceplaces.it/siena/porta-ovile)
- Conflict on the fresco's location: guides and Wikipedia place it on the gate; a TripAdvisor review says it was moved for protection to the nearby Oratory of San Rocco. Unconfirmed; check before showing the fresco in the illustration. A tourist page that dates the Sano di Pietro fresco to the 14th century is wrong on its face (the painter worked in the 15th century). — [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g187902-d25163941-Reviews-Porta_Ovile-Siena_Tuscany.html); [GPSmyCity](https://www.gpsmycity.com/attractions/porta-ovile-(ovile-gate)-32607.html)
- Events: walled up in the siege ("During the 1554 siege Porta Ovile and Porta Fontebranda were walled up to strengthen the walls"; the contrada says 1555, walled and partly buried); considerable damage in an earthquake in 1798. Confidence: siege closure well documented (year differs), earthquake single source. — [Hotel Minerva, Porta Ovile](https://www.albergominerva.it/en/porta-ovile-a-siena/); [nobilcontradadelbruco.it, Il Territorio](https://www.nobilcontradadelbruco.it/il-territorio/); [GPSmyCity](https://www.gpsmycity.com/attractions/porta-ovile-(ovile-gate)-32607.html)
- Name: explained as a watered resting place for flocks during transhumance (gate "del Gregge" or "Ovile"). Confidence: tradition or legend. — [Niceplaces](https://www.niceplaces.it/siena/porta-ovile)
- Fonte d'Ovile (just outside the gate): a disused medieval fountain, creation date given as 1260 (Google Arts & Culture), "1262" (Frommer's) or late 13th century; it "preserves three large ogival arches, two on the front and one on the side"; never monumentalised and practically unchanged since the Middle Ages; it lost importance in the 14th century when the Fonte Nuova d'Ovile was built inside the walls (a plaque shows that fountain working by 1298). New water veins found in 1274 led to Fontenuova and Fonte d'Ovile. Confidence: well documented for existence and form, date varies. — [Google Arts & Culture, Fonte d'Ovile](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/fonte-d-ovile/WQGDgTA0ll4XjA); [Hotel Minerva, historic fountains](https://www.albergominerva.it/le-fonti-storiche-di-siena-itinerario-tra-fontebranda-fonte-nuova-e-fonte-di-ovile/); [Wikipedia, Bottini of Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottini_of_Siena); [Cityzeum, Fonte Nuova d'Ovile](https://www.cityzeum.com/fonte-nuova-dovile)
- Contrada: inside the Bruco's listed territory (see part 2). Confidence: well documented. — [nobilcontradadelbruco.it, La Contrada](https://www.nobilcontradadelbruco.it/la-contrada/)

**Porta Camollia**
- State catalogue: a gate attributable to the 14th century, "ricostruita nel 1604 su disegno di Alessandro Casolani e inaugurata in onore di Ferdinando I dei Medici". Confidence: well documented. — [ICCD record 9706061](https://sigecweb.beniculturali.it/sigec/item/print/ICCD9706061)
- Official tourism site: design by Alessandro Casolani, travertine sculpture by Domenico Cafaggi called Capo; the Medici coat of arms is held up by two allegorical figures; the motto is a tribute to Grand Duke Ferdinando I, who came in 1604 for the inauguration, and echoes an older Latin saying, "Ianua patet Cor magis". Confidence: well documented. — [Terre di Siena, Terzo di Camollia](https://www.terredisiena.it/cammini-outdoor/4-5-la-storia-sui-muri-terzo-di-camollia/)
- Inscription: "Cor magis tibi Sena pandit", rendered "Siena shows a heart that is bigger than this gate" (literally, Siena opens its heart to you wider than this gate); placed to record Ferdinando I's entry; the outer face carries the Medici shield on a stone arch added in 1604; the gate opens into the Contrada dell'Istrice; the earlier gate was razed in the 1555 siege. Confidence: well documented for text, date and meaning; the link to a specific visit is called traditional by some. — [Wikipedia, Porta Camollia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Camollia,_Siena); [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/porta-camollia/1574796)
- Outer works: the Republic commissioned Baldassarre Peruzzi to build the outer fortification in 1527, finished 1532; a separate catalogue record for the Antiporto di Camollia gives a construction date of 1270. The two may describe different structures. Confidence: single source each. — [Terre di Siena, Francigena urbana](https://www.terredisiena.it/en/trekking-and-outdoor/2-5-on-the-francigena-urbana/); [ICCD record 10115586](https://sigecweb.beniculturali.it/sigec/item/print/ICCD10115586)
- Doubtful claims to ignore: a Google Arts & Culture caption crediting Peruzzi for the 1604 gate; a travel page dating the gate to the 7th century. — [Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/porta-camollia-baldassarre-peruzzi/KwFCFOEaK9zF7A)

**Porta Romana (brief)**
- Built 1327-1328 by Agnolo di Ventura and Agostino di Giovanni; crenellated roofline with machicolation; two portals with a small inner court between them; a hotel guide dates the original to 1326, calls it the largest gate in Siena and mentions an antemurale with two stone she-wolves attributed to Giovanni di Stefano. In 1417 Taddeo di Bartolo was commissioned to paint the Madonna on the inner portal; the work was continued by Sassetta and completed by Sano di Pietro (1466 per one account; the detached Coronation in San Francesco is dated 1447-1450). Confidence: well documented for date and builders; "largest" is a single promotional source. — [Wikipedia, Porta Romana, Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Romana,_Siena); [Hotel Minerva, Porta Romana](https://www.albergominerva.it/en/porta-romana/)

**Porta Pispini (brief)**
- In 1530-31 Sodoma painted a Nativity on its inner face; the fragments are now kept at San Francesco (Wikipedia dates the fresco 1531). Confidence: well documented for the fresco. — [Hotel Minerva, historic gates (IT)](https://www.albergominerva.it/porta-camollia-e-le-altre-porte-storiche-di-siena/); [Wikipedia, Basilica of San Francesco, Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Francesco,_Siena)

### Inferences
- Natural gate for Via dei Rossi: Porta Ovile. It is the gate of the same contrada (Bruco), it sits in the same north-east sector below San Francesco, and the Bando's Bruco clause literally starts from it. Porta Camollia has the better-known welcome motto and lies on the Francigena axis that passes the Arco dei Rossi, but it is the Istrice's gate; using it for a Bruco-side business would be a contrada mismatch that Sienese visitors would notice.
- Illustrator brief supported by the sources: brick construction; one large pointed (ogival) arch; a crenellated forework in front of the gate so that the entrance reads as two successive walls (Frommer's "double" gate); squat, low proportions rather than a tall tower; a small tabernacle with a Madonna and Child on the inner wall of the forework; outside and below, the Fonte d'Ovile with two pointed arches on its front and one on its side. None of the sources gives the number of merlons, the merlon shape, arch counts on the forework or any tower height.
- A truthful one-line caption could read: "Porta Ovile, 13th-century gate of the Bruco, with its crenellated forework and the Madonna attributed to Sano di Pietro" (subject to the fresco-location check above).

### Gaps
- No measured description (merlon count and shape, forework arch form, heights, corbels) was found; the illustrator must work from photographs (the Google Arts & Culture asset and the Italian Wikipedia page both carry images) and ideally a site visit.
- Walking distance from Via dei Rossi 79/81 to Porta Ovile: not sourced.
- Whether the Sano di Pietro fresco is still in place, and its date: unresolved.
- Porta Pispini construction date, architecture and contrada, and Porta Romana's contrada: no extract retrieved (Italian Wikipedia pages exist: [Porta dei Pispini](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_dei_Pispini), [Porta Romana](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Romana_(Siena)), [Mura di Siena](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mura_di_Siena)).

## 4. Food history: dishes, products, truffles and the osteria

### Takeaway
Most of the menu rests on well-attested but mostly 19th-century written evidence, with older origins that are traditional rather than documented: dolceforte is recorded by Artusi with chocolate (so the chocolate version cannot be medieval), pici's Etruscan-tomb story is a legend, and "ribollita" as a name first appears in print in 1910. The firmly dated items are the protected products (Cinta Senese DOP 2012, Ricciarelli IGP 2010, Cantuccini Toscani IGP 2016), the 1338-39 Lorenzetti fresco showing a belted pig, the 1205 Montecelso record behind panforte, and Sienese tavern records from 1288, 1355 and 1786.

### Cited Findings

**Cinghiale in dolceforte**
- Artusi, recipe no. 285 "Cignale dolce-forte": the sauce uses "40 grammi di uva passolina, 30 grammi di cioccolata, 30 grammi di pinoli, 20 grammi di candito e 50 grammi di zucchero" with vinegar added sparingly; Artusi adds that a simpler version of sugar and vinegar alone will do. Confidence: well documented (19th-century printed recipe). — [Casa Artusi](https://ricette.casartusi.it/ricette/cignale-dolce-forte/)
- Regional tourism recipe: the dolceforte is "un condimento preparato mescolando l'uvetta, i pinoli, il cioccolato grattugiato, i canditi, lo zucchero, la farina, l'aceto e un po' d'acqua", added to the game in the last minutes of cooking; a lighter version uses only vinegar and sugar. Confidence: well documented. — [Visit Tuscany, cinghiale in dolceforte](https://www.visittuscany.com/it/ricette/la-ricetta-del-cinghiale-in-dolceforte/)
- Period: sources disagree. One calls it a "ricetta antichissima di origini medievali", others "un antico piatto rinascimentale toscano"; Visit Tuscany says "Florence was starting to experiment with cacao which had only just arrived in Italy, and dishes like dolceforte wild boar were popular". Confidence: Renaissance framing well documented in secondary sources; medieval claim tradition or legend. — [Visit Tuscany, dolceforte meat](https://www.visittuscany.com/en/recipes/dolceforte-meat/); [CiboToday](https://www.cibotoday.it/storie/storia-ricetta-carne-dolceforte-toscana.html)
- Chocolate is a later layer: the Accademia dei Georgofili writes that the arrival of cacao in Europe "definì anche la ricetta del dolce-forte anche se in origine era una salsa che veniva aggiunta a metà cottura di particolari umidi, come cinghiale e lepre"; one source says the Renaissance recipe used honey where chocolate is used now. Confidence: Georgofili single authoritative source; honey claim single source. — [Accademia dei Georgofili](https://georgofili.net/articoli/luso-del-cacao-in-cucina-il-dolce-e-forte/4654); [CiboToday](https://www.cibotoday.it/storie/storia-ricetta-carne-dolceforte-toscana.html)
- A recipe archive describes an older-style sauce built on "cioccolato, panforte, cavallucci tritati, uvetta, pinoli e noci" (i.e. crumbled Sienese sweets). Confidence: single source, attribution to confirm. — [Taccuini Gastrosofici](https://www.taccuinigastrosofici.it/ita/ricette/contemporanea/dcarni---pesci/Cinghiale-in-dolceforte.html)
- Dolceforte dishes (hare, boar, ox tongue) were special-occasion food and are now rare in restaurants. Confidence: single source. — [La Maggiorana Persa](https://lamaggioranapersa.com/?p=8271)

**Pici**
- Hand-rolled thick pasta from the province of Siena, of peasant origin, traditionally flour and water without eggs, still made in the Valdichiana and Val d'Orcia; listed among Tuscany's traditional agri-food products (PAT). Confidence: well documented. — [Wikipedia, Pici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pici); [Visit Tuscany, Valdichiana senese](https://www.visittuscany.com/it/idee/sapori-della-valdichiana-senese/)
- Name: most often linked to the dialect verb "appiciare", the two-handed rolling of a piece of dough on a wooden board into a long thick string; alternatives are "appiccicare" (to stick) and a Monte Amiata verb "appicciare". Confidence: well documented as the common explanation, etymology debated (which page carries the "appiciare" sentence to confirm). — [Gambero Rosso](https://www.gamberorosso.it/notizie/storie/pasta-fatta-in-casa-con-i-consigli-degli-esperti-la-ricetta-dei-pici); [Chef's Mandala](https://chefsmandala.com/archaeology-pasta-pici/); [Verdidea Magazine](https://magazine.verdidea.com/it/blog/origine-e-storia-dei-pici-piatto-toscano-tra-i-piu-apprezzati-che-meriterebbe-il-riconoscimento-unes-159)
- Etruscan claim: some writers say pici appear in the banquet scene of the Tomba dei Leopardi at Tarquinia (a servant carrying a bowl of long irregular pasta); one source itself calls the Etruscan origin "more romantic than documented". The tomb dates to about 470-450 BC. Confidence: tradition or legend. — [Turismo.it](https://www.turismo.it/idee-di-viaggio/enogastronomia-vino/pici-toscani-parenti-rustici-degli-spaghetti-id-21259/); [Wikipedia, Tomb of the Leopards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_the_Leopards)
- Roman claim: the name is also attributed to the gastronome Marcus Gavius Apicius. Confidence: tradition or legend. — [Verdidea Magazine](https://magazine.verdidea.com/it/blog/origine-e-storia-dei-pici-piatto-toscano-tra-i-piu-apprezzati-che-meriterebbe-il-riconoscimento-unes-159)
- Modern record: Giovanni Righi Parenti called pici distant relatives of spaghetti in "La cucina toscana in 800 ricette tradizionali"; Celle sul Rigo was the first town to dedicate a festival to pici, about fifty years ago, held on the last weekend of May. Confidence: single source. — [Turismo.it](https://www.turismo.it/idee-di-viaggio/enogastronomia-vino/pici-toscani-parenti-rustici-degli-spaghetti-id-21259/)
- Classic pairings named in the result set: pici all'aglione and pici cacio e pepe. Confidence: well documented as dishes; no history retrieved. — [Ciao Toscana, pici all'aglione](https://www.ciaotoscana.it/cucina-toscana/pici-allaglione-la-ricetta-originale/); [Dissapore, pici cacio e pepe](https://www.dissapore.com/ricette/pici-cacio-e-pepe/)

**Ribollita**
- The name is recent: its first written appearance is given as 1910, in Alberto Cougnet's "L'arte cucinaria in Italia"; Artusi earlier described the same family of soup as "Zuppa toscana di magro alla contadina"; the name comes from reheating ("re-boiling") a soup made in quantity. Core ingredients: cavolo nero, other vegetables, dried cannellini beans and stale unsalted bread. Claims of a medieval birth are made without documents. Confidence: name and date well documented in secondary sources; medieval origin tradition or legend. — [Gazzetta del Gusto](https://gazzettadelgusto.it/cibo/ribollita-toscana-storia-origini-natura/?print=pdf); [Wikipedia, Ribollita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribollita); [Visit Italy](https://www.visititaly.eu/it/cibo-e-sapori/ribollita-ricetta-zuppa-toscana)

**Crostini neri**
- Chicken-liver spread on toasted bread with capers and anchovies; some versions add spleen (milza) and minced beef; "neri" refers to the very dark colour; an older family method cooks the spread for at least two hours. Etruscan-origin statements come without sources. Confidence: recipe well documented; origin tradition or legend. — [Visit Tuscany, crostini neri](https://www.visittuscany.com/it/ricette/crostini-neri-toscani/); [Cinelli Colombini](https://www.cinellicolombini.it/en/farm-experiences/black-crostini-typical-tuscan-appetizer/); [Firenze Made in Tuscany](https://www.firenzemadeintuscany.com/it/articolo/la-ricetta-infallibile-dei-crostini-neri-fegatini-di-pollo/)

**Trippa and the Palio**
- A 2010 food article describes contrada custom: after a good-luck "cenino" the tripe is prepared for the next morning; on the morning the horses are assigned the contrada meets at about 7:00 and tripe is served for breakfast, since lunch will be skipped. Confidence: single source (informal). — [Scatti di Gusto](https://www.scattidigusto.it/un-posto-a-tavola-al-palio-di-siena-per-il-ministro-brambilla)

**Cinta Senese**
- Ambrogio Lorenzetti's Allegory and Effects of Good and Bad Government in the Palazzo Pubblico is dated 1338-1339; a pig with a white belt (the "cinta") appears in the Effects of Good Government in the Countryside. Confidence: well documented. — [Wikipedia, The Allegory of Good and Bad Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Allegory_of_Good_and_Bad_Government); [Wikipedia, Cinta Senese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinta_Senese); [Finestre sull'Arte](https://www.finestresullarte.info/opere-e-artisti/una-porta-aperta-sulla-campagna-effetti-del-buon-governo-che-lorenzetti-dipinse-siena)
- "Cinta Senese" was entered in the EU register of protected designations of origin by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 217/2012 of 13 March 2012; the consortium, formed to save the breed from extinction, dates the recognition 14 March 2012; the specification was amended by Regulation (EU) 2020/412. The meat must come from animals whose parents are both entered in the breed's register or herdbook. Confidence: well documented (Wikipedia's "DOP since 2006 as Suino Cinto Toscano" and a regional list's "271/2012" conflict with the EU text). — [Regulation (EU) No 217/2012](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2012/217/data.htm?view=plain); [EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2020/412](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2020/412/oj); [AIS Lombardia, Cinta Senese](https://aislombardia.it/media2/libreria/allegati/%20ViniPlus27_CintaSenese.pdf)
- The breed is tied to the hills of the Montagnola Senese; statements that Etruscans and Romans already raised it are popular accounts. Confidence: origin area well documented; antiquity tradition or legend. — [AIS Lombardia, Cinta Senese](https://aislombardia.it/media2/libreria/allegati/%20ViniPlus27_CintaSenese.pdf)

**Bistecca alla fiorentina**
- Cut from the loin (lombata) of a young steer or heifer, with the T-shaped bone in the middle; usually Chianina; grilled over charcoal and served rare; thickness given as at least 4 cm and 800 g, or "three to four fingers". Confidence: well documented. — [Wikipedia, Bistecca alla fiorentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bistecca_alla_fiorentina); [Great Italian Chefs](https://www.greatitalianchefs.com/features/bistecca-alla-fiorentina-tuscany-steak)
- Breeds: one source insists on Chianina only, another accepts Calvana and Maremmana; there is no binding production specification, and Gambero Rosso reports an effort toward a traditional-speciality recognition with the breed question open. The dish was added to Italy's list of traditional food products in 2021. The word "bistecca" was borrowed from English "beefsteak" in the early 19th century; the link to the feast of San Lorenzo is the customary origin story. Confidence: status and etymology single source each; San Lorenzo story tradition or legend. — [Gambero Rosso](https://www.gamberorosso.it/notizie/attualita/bistecca-alla-fiorentina-riconoscimento-specilita-tradizionale/); [Wikipedia, Bistecca alla fiorentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bistecca_alla_fiorentina)

**Sienese sweets**
- Panforte: "I primi esempi di panforte sono quei pani ricchi di pepe e miele che una cronaca del 1205 cita come doni che servi e coloni erano obbligati a portare alle monache dell'Abbazia di Montecelso"; first factory 1810; in Artusi's 1891 cookbook; the IGP production area is the whole province of Siena, in white (sugar-dusted) and black types. Panforte Margherita is said to date from Queen Margherita's 1879 visit for the Palio. Confidence: 1205 record and IGP well documented; Margherita story single source. — [Visit Tuscany, Panforte di Siena IGP](https://www.visittuscany.com/it/sapori/panforte-di-siena-igp/); [Wikipedia, Panforte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panforte); [Aleteia](https://aleteia.org/2023/12/04/the-catholic-origins-of-sienas-christmas-cake)
- Ricciarelli: PGI obtained in March 2010, the first Italian baked confectionery product so protected; production limited to the province of Siena; origin placed by food historians in the 14th century; the story that the crusader Ricciardetto della Gherardesca brought them back to his castle near Volterra is a legend. Confidence: IGP well documented; Ricciardetto tradition or legend. — [Wikipedia, Ricciarelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricciarelli); [Visit Tuscany, Ricciarelli di Siena PGI](https://www.visittuscany.com/en/flavors/ricciarelli-di-siena-pgi/)
- Cavallucci: the originals were flour, honey, sugar and anise seed without today's candied fruit and nuts; an older name, "berriquocoli", is tied (with a "probabilmente") to about 1515. Confidence: single source. — [Rivista di Agraria](https://www.rivistadiagraria.org/articoli/anno-2021/dolci-natalizi-storia-leggenda/?print=pdf)
- Cantucci: "Cantuccini Toscani" / "Cantucci Toscani" IGP, national decree published in the Gazzetta Ufficiale no. 40 of 18 February 2016 (EU listing January 2016); almonds at least 20 percent; production across Tuscany; traditionally dipped in vin santo; the consortium took its present form on 8 September 2023. A Medici-court origin is asserted by tourism pages without a document. Confidence: IGP well documented; Medici origin tradition or legend. — [Visit Tuscany, Cantuccini Toscani IGP](https://www.visittuscany.com/en/flavors/pgi-cantuccini-toscani/); [MIPAAF decree, 2016](https://www.unionalimentari.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/MIPAAF-Provv-29.01.2016-Iscrizione-Cantucci-Toscani-IGP-COMPL.pdf); [La Nazione](https://www.lanazione.it/firenze/cronaca/il-consorzio-dei-cantuccini-toscani-igp-ottiene-dal-masaf-il-riconoscimento-della-tutela-e7b547b8)

**Pecorino**
- Pecorino Toscano is a firm ewe's-milk cheese with DOP status (1996 per Wikipedia; a university record says 1987, to confirm). Pecorino di Pienza has no DOP of its own; only the barrique-aged version is on the regional traditional-products list. The pecorino of the Crete Senesi was already prized in the mid-1400s by Lorenzo il Magnifico and Pope Pius II Piccolomini; the cheese was once called "cacio marzolino" because cheesemaking began in March. Confidence: DOP well documented (year to confirm); 15th-century anecdote single source. — [Wikipedia, Pecorino toscano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_toscano); [ONAF](https://www.onaf.it/uploads/public/11798_pecorino-toscano.pdf); [Scatti di Gusto, Pecorino di Pienza](https://www.scattidigusto.it/pecorino-di-pienza-6-stili-per-apprezzare-il-formaggio-della-val-dorcia); [Devour Tours](https://devourtours.com/blog/tuscan-cheese/)

**Truffles of the Crete Senesi**

Confidence: well documented. — [Visit Tuscany, 2026 fair](https://www.visittuscany.com/it/eventi/san-giovanni-d-asso-mostra-mercato-del-tartufo-bianco-novembre-2026/); [MeteoWeb](https://www.meteoweb.eu/2018/11/mostra-mercato-tartufo-bianco-crete-senesi/1178695/); [Itinerari nel Gusto, Marzuolo](https://www.itinerarinelgusto.it/sagre-e-feste/festa-del-tartufo-marzuolo-san-giovanni-d-asso-1750)

Confidence: well documented. — [Regione Toscana, nuova disciplina tartufi](https://www.regione.toscana.it/-/tartufi-nuova-disciplina-per-ricerca-raccolta-e-coltivazione); [Regione Toscana, FAQ](https://www.regione.toscana.it/-/domande-frequenti-4)

**What an osteria was**
- Words: "oste" comes through Old French from Latin "hospitem" (hospes, both host and guest); "taverna" from Latin "taberna"; an osteria is defined as a modest, popular place that sells wine by the glass and often serves simple meals. Confidence: well documented. — [Accademia della Crusca](https://accademiadellacrusca.it/sites/www.accademiadellacrusca.it/files/page/2012/10/27/lospitalita_e_vera_se_ricambiata_-_frati_e_iannizzotto_-_pretesti_2012_-_numero_8__40-42.pdf); [Wiktionary, osteria](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/osteria)
- Medieval pattern: wine-selling taverns were distinct from inns with lodging, though the medieval "hospitium" often combined beds and wine, which is why innkeepers and taverners frequently shared one guild; such houses needed a licence. Confidence: single source each (general, not Siena-specific). — [Italia Medievale](https://www.italiamedievale.org/gli-osti-e-gli-albergatori-negli-statuti-comunali-di-bevagna/); [Mondo Padano](https://www.mondopadano.it/news/cultura-spettacoli/nelle-movimentate-osterie-medievali/2797)
- Siena, documented: a local-history study (Orlando Papei and Luigi Bichi, "Osterie e locande dei secoli passati") records the Osteria della Stella as "un'antichissima osteria, tanto che si trova pure nell'elenco compilato nel 1288 dal comune", i.e. the Comune compiled a list of osterie in 1288; names usually matched the painted sign ("in genere il nome si rispecchiava nell'insegna"); guests were mainly merchants, pilgrims, the sick awaiting shelter and ordinary travellers, but also swindlers and prostitutes; a 1667 notice required anyone keeping an inn or lodging to register with the guild's camarlengo and pay a half-yearly fee. Confidence: single source (local study citing archives). — [ilpalio.org, Osterie e locande (PDF)](https://www.ilpalio.org/osterie.pdf); [ilpalio.org, page 2](https://www.ilpalio.org/pagina2.htm)
- Siena, statute: a statute of the innkeepers, lodging-keepers and taverners of Siena dated 1355 survives in the Archivio di Stato di Siena (Arti, 42); the standard study is M. Tuliani, "Osti, avventori e malandrini" (1994), on inns and taverns in Siena and its countryside in the 14th and 15th centuries. Confidence: single source, cited second-hand (which of the two linked papers carries the citation to confirm). — [CORE paper](https://files01.core.ac.uk/download/141655593.pdf); [Firenze University Press journal](https://oajournals.fupress.net/index.php/ds/article/download/330/328/296)
- Siena, 1786: 130 osterie and bettole in the city and its Masse, among them one in "via dell'arco dei Rossi", a Locanda della Giraffa in the parish of San Pietro a Ovile and a bettola in "via d'Uvile a S. Rocco 76". Confidence: single source. — [ilpalio.org, 1786 list](https://www.ilpalio.org/notizie459.htm)

### Inferences

The recipe as cooked today, with chocolate, is attested in print in the 19th century (Artusi) and described by the Georgofili as shaped by the arrival of cacao; safer wording is "a sweet-and-sour recipe of Renaissance tradition, later enriched with chocolate".

## 5. Wine geography from Siena

### Takeaway
The appellations on the list all have firm legal dates and grape rules from their consortia: Chianti Classico (Sangiovese 80-100 percent, DOCG 1984, own rules since 1996, zone first delimited in 1716), Vino Nobile di Montepulciano (DOCG 1980, Sangiovese at least 70 percent), Brunello di Montalcino (DOCG 1980, 100 percent Sangiovese, released in the fifth year) and Vernaccia di San Gimignano (first Italian DOC in 1966, DOCG 1993). Distances from Siena were not sourced in this session.

### Cited Findings

**Chianti Classico DOCG**
- Sangiovese 80 to 100 percent with up to 20 percent other permitted red grapes; Gran Selezione requires 90 to 100 percent Sangiovese (2023 code). Confidence: well documented. — [Consorzio Chianti Classico, Production Code 2023](https://www.chianticlassico.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Production-Code-2023.pdf)
- History: Cosimo III's edict of 1716 fixed the boundaries of the production zone (the consortium calls it the first wine area in the world defined by law); consortium founded 14 May 1924 by 33 producers; the suffix "Classico" added by ministerial decree in 1932; DOC 1967; DOCG 1984; its own separate production code by decree of 5 August 1996. The black rooster (Gallo Nero) is the trademark, linked by the consortium to the area since the 14th century, with its own foundation legend. Confidence: dates well documented; rooster story tradition or legend. — [Consorzio Chianti Classico, History](https://www.chianticlassico.com/en/consortium/history-of-chianti-classico/); [La Leggenda del Gallo Nero](https://leggenda.chianticlassico.com/en/)

**Chianti DOCG and Chianti Colli Senesi**
- In the Chianti DOCG rules (decree of 19 July 2017) the Colli Senesi sub-zone requires Sangiovese 75 to 100 percent; Riserva needs 13.0 percent alcohol for Colli Senesi against 12.5 for other sub-zones; DOC 1967, DOCG 1984. Confidence: well documented. — [Consorzio Vino Chianti, Disciplinare DM 19.7.17](https://www.consorziovinochianti.it/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Disciplinare-di-produzione-vini-Chianti-docg-DM-19.7.17.pdf); [Wine with Seth, Colli Senesi](https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/chianti-colli-senesi-sub-zone/)
- The sub-zone covers part of the province of Siena (one page says 15 of 35 comuni, in three non-contiguous districts). A page claiming a 2026 rule change (minimum 60 percent, eight sub-zones) could not be confirmed. Confidence: single source; treat the 2026 claim as to confirm. — [Chateau Monty, Chianti Colli Senesi](https://chateaumonty.com/chianti-colli-senesi-docg)

**Vino Nobile di Montepulciano DOCG and Rosso di Montepulciano DOC**
- Vino Nobile: DOCG in force from 1980 (decree of 1 July 1980), described by its consortium as the first Italian wine to receive the classification; Sangiovese (locally Prugnolo Gentile) minimum 70 percent; at least two years' ageing counted from 1 January after the harvest; Riserva at least three years and 12.5 percent; consortium founded 1965. Confidence: well documented. — [Consorzio del Vino Nobile, Disciplinaries](https://www.consorziovinonobile.it/disciplinaries/); [Consorzio del Vino Nobile](https://www.consorziovinonobile.it/consorzio-montepulciano/); [Wikipedia, Vino Nobile di Montepulciano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vino_Nobile_di_Montepulciano)
- Rosso di Montepulciano: DOC rules approved by presidential decree of 21 December 1988; Sangiovese minimum 70 percent; released from 1 March of the year after the harvest. Confidence: well documented. — [Consorzio del Vino Nobile, Disciplinaries](https://www.consorziovinonobile.it/disciplinaries/); [Italian Wine Central](https://italianwinecentral.com/denomination/rosso-di-montepulciano-doc/)

**Brunello di Montalcino DOCG and Rosso di Montalcino DOC**
- Brunello: DOCG 1980 (Wikipedia calls it the first Italian wine to receive the designation, a claim the Vino Nobile consortium also makes; give both); 100 percent Sangiovese, called Brunello locally; released from 1 January of the fifth year after the harvest, with at least two years in wood and four months in bottle. Confidence: well documented. — [Consorzio del Vino Brunello di Montalcino, Wines](https://www.consorziobrunellodimontalcino.it/en/583/wines); [Brunello disciplinare (PDF)](https://www.consorziobrunellodimontalcino.it/files/Disciplinari/disciplinare-bdm-19-a4-bdm.pdf); [Wikipedia, Brunello di Montalcino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunello_di_Montalcino)
- Rosso di Montalcino: DOC recognised by presidential decree of 25 November 1983, first DOC vintage 1984; released from 1 September of the year after the harvest. Confidence: well documented. — [Consorzio del Vino Brunello di Montalcino, Wines](https://www.consorziobrunellodimontalcino.it/en/583/wines); [Italian Wine Central](https://italianwinecentral.com/denomination/rosso-di-montalcino-doc/)

**Vernaccia di San Gimignano DOCG**
- White wine from the Vernaccia di San Gimignano grape; first Italian DOC in 1966 (decree of 3 March 1966, published 6 May 1966); DOCG by decree of 9 July 1993; earliest documentary mention 1276, in San Gimignano's tax ordinances setting a duty on exported Vernaccia; consortium founded 1972. Minimum share of Vernaccia: Wikipedia says 90 percent, the figure in the brief (85 percent) was not confirmed. Confidence: dates well documented; grape percentage to confirm. — [Wine with Seth, Vernaccia di San Gimignano](https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/vernaccia-di-san-gimignano-docg/?path=tuscany); [Dissapore](https://www.dissapore.com/bere/vino/scoprire-la-vernaccia-di-san-gimignano/); [Wikipedia, Vernaccia di San Gimignano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernaccia_di_San_Gimignano)

### Inferences
- All four reds on the osteria's list are Sangiovese wines under different local names (Sangiovese, Prugnolo Gentile, Brunello), which gives the wine page a simple true thread: one grape, four hill towns around Siena.
- "Chianti" on the menu may mean Chianti DOCG (including Colli Senesi, the Sienese sub-zone) or Chianti Classico DOCG (black rooster); they are legally separate appellations since 1996, so copy must name the one actually poured.
- "Montalcino" on the menu should be resolved to Brunello or Rosso di Montalcino, and "first DOCG" should not be claimed for either Brunello or Vino Nobile without the qualifier "among the first, in 1980".

### Gaps
- Distance in kilometres from Siena to the Chianti Classico zone, Montepulciano, Montalcino and San Gimignano: not sourced.
- One-line tasting character for each wine from a consortium page: not retrieved.
- Current Vernaccia di San Gimignano grape minimum and any 2026 change to the Chianti DOCG rules: check the current disciplinari ([AIS, Vernaccia di San Gimignano](https://aisitalia.it/Disciplinari/Toscana/DOCG/Vernaccia%20di%20San%20Gimignano.pdf)).

## 6. Palio dates, restaurant demand and contrada dinners; UNESCO status

### Takeaway
The Palio is run on 2 July and 16 August (weather can push it back, as on both dates in 2026); the evening before each race every running contrada holds its Cena della Prova Generale for hundreds to thousands of people at street tables, and local reporting speaks of "eight days of Palio" as the peak of the city's summer trade. The Historic Centre of Siena has been a UNESCO World Heritage property since 1995.

### Cited Findings
- The Palio is held twice a year, on 2 July and 16 August; ten of the seventeen contrade run each race. Confidence: well documented. — [Wikipedia, Palio di Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palio_di_Siena); [Comune di Siena, Palio brochure](https://palio.comune.siena.it/sites/default/files/2025-07/Brochure%20il%20palio%20di%20siena_ENG%20WEB.pdf)
- 2026: rain moved the July race to 3 July (won by the Aquila, its first win in 34 years) and the August race to 18 August (won by the Nicchio). Confidence: well documented. — [AGI](https://www.agi.it/cronaca/news/2026-07-03/palio-siena-vince-aquila-37858257/); [Sky TG24](https://tg24.sky.it/cronaca/2026/08/18/palio-siena-2026-vincitore)
- Cena della Prova Generale: held in each contrada on the evening before the race (1 July and 15 August), after the general trial; tables are laid for up to a few thousand people; seats are limited and booked ahead with the contrada, by phone rather than email; sources differ on how open the dinners are to outsiders. Confidence: well documented as a custom; access rules vary. — [Scatti di Gusto, guida alle cene in contrada](https://www.scattidigusto.it/2017/08/04/palio-di-siena-cena-contrade-ferragosto/); [La Nazione, Palio 2019](https://www.lanazione.it/siena/cronaca/palio-siena-2019-ef7c176d)
- In the Bruco the dinner is reported at about a thousand covers in San Francesco ("Mille a cena in San Francesco"). Confidence: single source (headline level). — [La Nazione](https://www.lanazione.it/siena/cronaca/mille-a-cena-in-san-francesco-donnini-dal-9-il-bao-bello-grande-lavoro-dei-contradaioli-c96fd9a9)
- Demand: for one July Palio hotel room occupancy reached about 80 percent, with the full sell-out "tradizionalmente" recorded for the August race; that year nobody laid outdoor tables on the evening of 2 July because of rain. Confidence: single source (year to confirm). — [La Nazione, il meteo pesa sul business](https://www.lanazione.it/siena/cronaca/il-meteo-pesa-sul-business-i-conti-di-hotel-e-ristoranti-c9bf9450)
- A La Nazione report of 29 July 2026 says that outside "gli otto giorni di Palio" arrivals and stays in the city fell sharply, with one restaurateur calling it "veramente un'estate povera". Confidence: single source (URL attribution to confirm). — [La Nazione, turismo Siena](https://www.lanazione.it/siena/cronaca/turismo-siena-a3by5neg)
- Supply side: bars and restaurants in the historic centre rose from 228 in 2008 to 271 (Confcommercio study). Confidence: single source. — [gonews](https://www.gonews.it/?p=940457)
- UNESCO: "Historic Centre of Siena" was inscribed in 1995 under criteria (i), (ii) and (iv) (Committee decision 19 COM VIII.C.1); the Comune dates the inscription 5 December 1995 and marked thirty years on the List; UNESCO's listing gives 170 ha for the property and 9,907 ha for the buffer zone. Confidence: well documented. — [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, list 717](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/717/); [UNESCO decision 19 COM VIII.C.1](https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/3087/); [Comune di Siena](https://www.comune.siena.it/novita/siena-celebra-i-trentanni-nella-lista-del-patrimonio-mondiale-unesco)

### Inferences
- For a restaurant between Banchi di Sopra and San Francesco the Palio calendar cuts both ways: the four days before each race bring the year's densest footfall, but on the eve of the race much of the local clientele eats at the contrada tables (the Bruco's are laid next door in San Francesco), and street access changes. The website should carry a dated Palio notice (opening hours, booking, access) rather than a generic promise.
- Because races can slip by one to two days for rain (both did in 2026), any Palio-week offer or booking policy should be worded around "the days of the Palio", not fixed dates.
