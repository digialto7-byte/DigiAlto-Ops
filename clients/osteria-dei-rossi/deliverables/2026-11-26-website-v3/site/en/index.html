The story starts here

Why come here

  • Opened in 1992. We are in the Contrada del Bruco, in Siena.

  • Inside the walls, about 5 minutes' walk from Piazza del Campo.

  • A room of beams, brick vaults and terracotta floors.

  • Listed by Gambero Rosso among Siena's restaurants for meat.

I · The house

Here you feel at home

Osteria dei Rossi is a warm place and the service is very friendly: when you walk in, someone is there to welcome you.

Beams overhead, wood on the walls, terracotta underfoot. Brick arches, wrought-iron lanterns, green-and-white check napkins. From the counter you look into the kitchen; in the kitchen is Luca.

II · The contrada

Since 1992 in the Bruco contrada

The osteria is in the Contrada del Bruco, on Via dei Rossi in Siena. The contrada's only city gate is Porta Ovile. Giuseppe "Beppe" Carrieri, a brucaiolo (a man of the Bruco contrada), opened the osteria. On the walls hang photographs of the contrada and of Siena, as inspiration.

Since early 2024 it has been run by Luca and Max, with Luca in the kitchen.

III · The table

Typical Sienese cooking with medieval roots

Slow-cooked stews, a sweet-and-sour sauce, polenta alongside: cooking from another age. The stews come to the table in a terracotta bowl, the coccio. To begin, crostini neri senesi, a starter that carries the name of the city.

  • Wild boar
  • Pici
  • Cinta
  • Truffle
  • Porcini
  • Fiorentina
  • Cantucci

Wild boar in dolceforte

Wild boar in dolceforte comes in a two-handled terracotta bowl, dark and glossy, with a bay leaf. Dolceforte is a sweet-and-sour sauce that goes back to medieval cooking; the chocolate in today's recipe came in later centuries.

Wild boar in dolceforte, on the menu

Wild boar in the coccio

Cinghiale al coccio is wild boar in umido, served in its own coccio, with polenta alongside. A rich plate: if you want side dishes, you order them separately.

IV · The pici

Pici in Siena a pasta of the Siena area

Pici are thick strands of pasta, just flour and water, rolled by hand and typical of the Siena area.

Here they come with the white Cinta Senese ragù, which has no tomato, or with duck ragù: the two the Oste recommends. Then cacio e pepe, or wild boar ragù. Four ways, one visit for each.

V · The larder

Cinta Senese, truffle, porcini from the osteria's larder

The Cinta Senese with the white band

The Cinta Senese is the native pig breed of the Siena hills: dark, with a white band, the "cinta", around its shoulders. Ambrogio Lorenzetti painted it in his fresco of 1338–39 in the Palazzo Pubblico, and it has DOP (protected-origin) status. Here it becomes Tegamata, a slow stew with no tomato that the Oste recommends, or the Tagliere del Cacciatore.

The Tegamata di Cinta, on the menu

The truffle fresh, all year round

By the time the plate arrives, the scent has already warned you. Fresh truffle is sliced in thin shavings over the tagliatelle and the grilled fillet, and it also comes on crostoni with gorgonzola.

Fillet with truffle, on the menu

Porcini mushrooms fresh, in three ways

Three ways, one mushroom. Fresh porcini come on tagliatelle, grilled, or fried, as a side dish.

Tagliatelle with porcini, on the menu

The spaghetti alla chitarra

Spaghetti alla chitarra with the osteria's sauce are dressed with tomato, gorgonzola and chilli: a bold flavour, red and spicy. If it is your first visit, you can begin here.

Spaghetti alla chitarra, on the menu

The boards and the crostoni

The boards come on slabs of wood: the gran tagliere of cured meats, or the Tagliere del Contadino, with honey and preserves beside the Tuscan cheeses. Then the Bis di crostoni: gorgonzola and truffle, or cheese, pears and honey. Beside it, a glass of local wine.

The boards, on the menu

The fiorentina by weight

Every week Alessio, our trusted butcher, chooses the selection: different cuts, from different breeds. The bistecca alla fiorentina is cooked on the grill and priced by the weight you choose. It is one of the dishes the Oste recommends. The price per 100 g is on the menu.

VI · The sweet

Cantucci and Vin Santo

To end the meal, Tuscan tradition calls for cantucci, almond biscuits, dipped in Vin Santo, the sweet Tuscan wine.

A cantuccio, a dip in the glass, a bite: no hurry. Here they come on a plate, with a small glass of Vin Santo.

All the desserts on the menu

VII · The cellar

Tuscan wines in the cellar, past the grotto

A grotto of successive brick arches and vaults leads to the cellar, where the wines of the list are kept. There are the great reds of the Siena area, and then Bolgheri, other Tuscan reds, whites and sparkling wines. Not sure what to choose? Ask us.

The great reds of the Siena area
